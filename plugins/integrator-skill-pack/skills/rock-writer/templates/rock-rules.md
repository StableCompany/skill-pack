# Rock rules

Run every check on every rock. Each check says what to look for, the flag to raise, the fix to suggest, and where the rule comes from. "Katie" is Katie Sandquist, who held the Integrator seat and co-wrote this pack. "Ours" means The Stable Company's, built from Katie's list.

A flag is a question for the user, never a verdict. The user can fix it, or keep the rock as it is and give a reason, which goes on the rock as "kept on purpose". Lines the user adds under "My own rules" in `CLAUDE.md` win over these checks, except never invent.

## 1. One owner

- **Check:** one person's name.
- **Flags:** "Two owners" or "No owner".
- **Fix:** ask which one person drives it to done. The others go under Depends on or Help needed. Never pick the owner.
- **Source:** Katie's weekly meeting teaching: if more than one person is accountable, no one is.

## 2. Everyone has a number

- **Check:** done is a number, or a test anyone could answer with yes or no on the due date.
- **Flag:** "Needs a number".
- **Fix:** ask what number or test would prove it done. Never supply the number; write `number?` until the user gives one.
- **Source:** Katie: everyone has a number, and every outcome is defined.

## 3. An outcome the owner controls

- **Check:** could the owner finish it by doing the work? Watch for an outcome that waits on someone outside saying yes: a candidate accepting, a customer signing, a vendor delivering, a boss approving.
- **Flag:** "Outside the owner's control".
- **Fix:** suggest the part the owner controls, with the number as a question. "Hire a supervisor" becomes "Hold <number?> interviews and make an offer by <date?>". "Get the budget approved" becomes "Present the budget by <date?>".
- **Source:** Katie: "hire someone" becomes "commit to N interviews."

## 4. Dependencies named up front

- **Check:** who the owner needs something from, what, and by when. Or "none", said by the user.
- **Flag:** "Depends on someone, not named", when the rock touches another seat, a vendor or a customer and nobody is named.
- **Fix:** ask the owner's dependencies and put them on the rock.
- **Source:** Katie: a rock that depends on someone else has to say so up front, so "I couldn't because Joey didn't give me my info" is known in week one, not at the end of the quarter.

## 5. The real working days

- **Check:** count the working days from the first day of the quarter (or today, once the quarter has started) to the due date: weekdays, minus days the company is closed, minus the owner's PTO, minus planning days. Show the math.
- **Flags:** "Due on a closed day", "Due during <owner>'s PTO", "Milestone on a closed day", "Milestone during PTO", or "Tight: <N> working days" when the user says the work needs more.
- **Fix:** offer the working day before, or ask for a new date.
- **Source:** Katie told each leader how many working days they really had in the quarter after holidays, PTO and planning sessions, sometimes about 60, and flagged rocks due on a holiday and milestones that ran into a vacation.

## 6. Feeds the plan for the year

- **Check:** the rock names the goal for the year it moves, or the issue it answers.
- **Flag:** "Ties to no goal", as a question.
- **Fix:** ask which goal it serves. If none, ask whether it belongs this quarter. A department rock should feed the company's plan, not only patch today's pain.
- **Source:** Katie: department rocks should feed the vision; rocks should be the ones that make a difference.

## 7. Dated milestones, one item each

- **Check:** milestones with dates, spread so something lands every month, each one a single item.
- **Flags:** "No milestones", "Milestone with no date", "Milestone holds several things".
- **Fix:** ask the owner for the steps and dates. Split a bundled milestone into single items.
- **Source:** Katie's planning practice: goals for the year break into quarterly rocks, and rocks into monthly milestones; her quarterly sessions settle on one owner, a clear finish line and single-item milestones per rock.

## 8. A rock, not a to-do

- **Check:** the work takes more than a week or two.
- **Flag:** "Looks like a to-do".
- **Fix:** ask whether to keep it as a rock or move it under Not rocks, for the to-do list.
- **Source:** ours, from Katie's weekly meeting teaching: rocks are 90-day priorities, to-dos are seven-day actions.

## 9. Not too many for one owner

- **Check:** count every rock each person owns this quarter, company and individual.
- **Flag:** "<Name> owns <N> rocks", when it is more than three.
- **Fix:** ask whether one can move, merge, or wait a quarter.
- **Source:** ours, from common EOS guidance (three to seven company rocks, and no more than about three for one person) and Katie's rule that rocks consider the other work on a person's plate. Change the number under "My own rules" if your team works differently.

## 10. A last read: SMART

- **Check:** specific, measurable, action-oriented, realistic and time-based. Checks 1 to 9 cover most of it. The last read: would someone outside the team know what done looks like, and when?
- **Source:** EOS practice, as Katie teaches it.
